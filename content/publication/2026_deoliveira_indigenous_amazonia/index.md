abstract: "Indigenous Lands are critical for protecting Indigenous Peoples and the Amazon. Yet, they are increasingly exposed to socio-environmental frontiers, bringing violence, illegal resource extraction and deforestation. Urgent action is required to prevent irreversible harm to both people and forests, particularly in Brazil's Vale do Javari Indigenous Land. Using satellite-based deforestation records from PRODES and MapBiomas, we show that forest loss surrounding the Vale do Javari Indigenous Land has risen sharply since 2008 while remaining stable within the territory itself, with the cumulative 200-km buffer zone recording nearly 187.5 times more forest loss than the Indigenous Land in 2022. We further find that 64% of deforestation in that buffer zone between 2008 and 2024 occurred within boundaries registered in Brazil's Rural Environmental Registry (CAR), a system originally designed for environmental enforcement but increasingly misused as evidence of possession over public lands. We call for legal protections to be extended to Indigenous Land buffer zones, for the designation of undesignated public forests as protected areas, and for strengthened enforcement capacity that supports Indigenous monitoring groups and protects Peoples living in voluntary isolation."

# Summary. An optional shortened abstract.
summary: "Indigenous Lands hold back Amazon deforestation, yet Brazil's buffer-zone protections cover conservation units and not Indigenous territories, exposing their edges to extraction and drug trafficking. Satellite forest-loss records from 2008 to 2024 were compared inside the Vale do Javari Indigenous Land, noted for its tri-border setting and its large population living in voluntary isolation, and in zones out to 200 kilometers. Clearing there surged from 2017 to 2022, in 2022 running nearly 187.5 times the loss inside the territory, and 64% fell within holdings on the national rural land registry, misused to claim public land. It eased in 2023 and 2024 yet stayed well above the previous decade."
